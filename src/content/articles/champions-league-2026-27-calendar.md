The 2026/27 Champions League started on 8 September and will not finish until 5 June. That is a nine-month competition, and this season it is stretched at both ends.

## Why there is a gap now

Matchday one was played on 8, 9 and 10 September. Matchday two does not arrive until 13 and 14 October — five weeks later, which is not how this competition usually breathes. In a normal year the first two rounds are a fortnight apart.

The reason is the summer. A World Cup year compresses the domestic calendars either side of it, and the continental competitions absorb the difference. So the eight matchdays of the league phase are pushed back and then run closer together: four rounds inside seven weeks between mid-October and late November, then December, then two in January.

The last of those is the one to note. Matchday eight, on 27 January, is played as a single round with all eighteen matches kicking off at the same time. That is deliberate. With one table of thirty-six teams rather than eight separate groups, a staggered final round would let the late kick-offs know exactly what result they needed. Simultaneous kick-offs remove that.

## A final in Madrid, on the wrong side of the city

The final is at the Estadio Metropolitano on Saturday 5 June 2027. It is worth being precise about what that means: the match is in Madrid, but the Metropolitano is Atlético's ground. Should Real Madrid reach it, they would play a European final in their own city, as visitors, at their rivals' stadium.

Paris Saint-Germain arrive as the holders, and as back-to-back winners after beating Arsenal on penalties in Budapest.

## What ten years of finals actually show

The table below is the last decade of finals, and it is less open than a 36-team competition sounds. Real Madrid won four of those ten and Paris Saint-Germain two, so six of the last ten went to two clubs. Liverpool, Bayern Munich, Chelsea and Manchester City took one each.

The losing side of the table is more interesting. Paris Saint-Germain lost the 2020 final and then won the next two they reached. Manchester City lost in 2021 and won in 2023. Liverpool are the only club to lose two in this span and still win one, beating Tottenham in 2019 between defeats by Real Madrid in 2018 and 2022. Bayern and Chelsea each won theirs and have not been back to a final since.

Inter have the worst of it: two finals, 2023 and 2025, both lost, the second by five goals. They opened this season at the Bernabéu and lost 2-1 there as well.
