A world championship decided by a single goal, and it took until the seventy-first minute to arrive. Finland beat Switzerland 1-0 in overtime in Zurich on 31 May, and the margin is the whole story of the game.

## Twenty-eight shots for one goal

Konsta Helenius scored 10:42 into overtime, from the right circle, past Leonardo Genoni. It was Finland's twenty-eighth shot of the night. Helenius is twenty and plays for the Buffalo Sabres; a player that age deciding a senior world final is unusual enough on its own.

At the other end, Justus Annunen faced twenty-two shots and stopped all of them. A shutout in a final is rare. A shutout in a final that goes to overtime means the goaltender held a scoreless game through more than seventy minutes of it.

Switzerland had the best chance of regulation and could not take it. A two-minute five-on-three power play began six seconds before the end of the first period — the sort of advantage that usually settles a tight game — and it produced nothing.

## Three finals, three defeats

This was Switzerland's third consecutive defeat in a world final. They were playing at home, in front of a Zurich crowd, against a team they had already lost finals to. There is no consolation in a one-goal overtime loss that does not also make the pattern worse.

Finland now have five titles: 1995, 2011, 2019, 2022 and this one. The route was not gentle either. They knocked out the Czech Republic 4-1 in the quarter-finals and then beat Canada 4-2 in the semi-final, which removed the tournament favourite before the final was even played.

## Norway's first medal, and Canada with nothing

Canada lost that semi-final and then lost the bronze game 3-2 in overtime to Norway. For Norway it is the first medal at an IIHF World Championship in the country's history. For Canada, the most decorated nation in the competition, it is a tournament that ended fourth.

## The best six of the tournament

Below is the media All-Star team — the six players the accredited journalists voted the best of the championship, shown on the ice in the positions they play.

It reads oddly against the result. Three of the six are Swiss, including Roman Josi, who was named tournament MVP and best defender while finishing as a runner-up. Only two are Finnish. The sixth is Macklin Celebrini of Canada, named best forward on fourteen points in ten games, from a team that did not medal.
