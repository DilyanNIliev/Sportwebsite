The 2026/27 Champions League league phase opened on Tuesday 8 September, and the tie everyone had circled on the calendar delivered what the draw promised: Real Madrid 2-1 Inter at the Santiago Bernabéu, decided inside the first half and defended for most of the second.

## Fourteen minutes, then a gift

Kylian Mbappé scored the opener after fourteen minutes. It was the kind of goal that has stopped being surprising — and it carried a milestone with it. The France forward is now level with Raúl on 71 Champions League goals, which places him fifth on the competition's all-time list. Raúl scored his in 142 appearances across a career built almost entirely at this club; that Mbappé has arrived at the same number is a reminder of how quickly he has been going about it.

The second goal was less about Madrid and more about Inter. Benjamin Pavard played a pass back towards his own goalkeeper. Martínez hesitated inside his six-yard box — a fraction of a second, no more — and Fede Valverde, Madrid's captain, took the ball off him and turned it into an empty net.

Two goals, both inside the first half, and neither of them the product of sustained pressure. Cristian Chivu's side had been the better team for stretches of that opening period and went in at the break two behind. That is a specific kind of frustrating: not outplayed, just punished.

## Inter's response fell short

Carlos Augusto pulled one back after the interval with a finish struck hard enough to change the temperature of the match. For a period afterwards Inter looked the side more likely to score again — Madrid dropped deeper, the midfield stopped stepping forward, and the Bernabéu grew noticeably quieter.

They did not score again. Madrid held on to a scoreline that had effectively been settled before half-time, and the final half hour was spent defending a lead built on two moments rather than ninety minutes of control.

For Inter, it is a defeat that will read worse in the table than it felt on the pitch. In an eight-match league phase, a narrow away loss at the Bernabéu is close to a neutral result. The concern is not the point total. It is that two errors cost them a game they were otherwise in.

## Mourinho's first European night, again

The result carried a subplot that will follow this Madrid team all season.

José Mourinho was appointed head coach in June 2026 on a three-year deal running to 2029, replacing Álvaro Arbeloa. It is a return to the club he left in 2013, delivered on a promise Florentino Pérez made during his re-election campaign, and taken up after two seasons in which Madrid won nothing. Mourinho left Benfica to take it. He is 63.

His first spell here, from 2010 to 2013, produced one La Liga title, one Copa del Rey and a Spanish Super Cup — a record that looks respectable in isolation and modest against the standard this particular club sets. It also produced three seasons of noise, which is part of why the appointment was popular with the members who voted for it and treated with caution by everyone else.

His first Champions League night back at the Bernabéu ended in a win over a side that has reached two of the last four finals. It also ended with his team defending for half an hour against a side reduced to chasing. Both of those observations will be made repeatedly over the coming months, usually by people who have already decided which one matters.

Erling Haaland scored both goals at Porto, in a fixture that had looked like one of the harder openers on City's schedule and turned out not to be. Dortmund and Betis each came through five-goal matches, which is the sort of thing the league phase produces when sides who have never met are drawn against each other with no second leg to plan around.

Aston Villa won away from home, which is rarer on an opening night than it looks. Away teams tend to arrive under-tested in September, and Villa's 3-2 at Club Brugge was the most useful three points of the evening in a format where the margin between eighth and ninth in the table decides whether you get a play-off round.

## What the table says

Very little, yet. The league phase runs to eight matches per team and concludes on 27 January with eighteen games played simultaneously — a scheduling decision designed to stop anyone knowing what result they need while they are playing for it.

One round in, the standings are noise. What matters is that Madrid have three points from a fixture that was always going to be among their hardest, and Inter have none from the same match without having played badly. Both of those facts will look different in January.
