Nikola Tsolov came to Monza leading the Formula 2 championship and left still leading it. Between those two facts sat a weekend that did him no favours whatsoever.

The tenth round of the season, held at the Autodromo Nazionale di Monza from 4 to 6 September in support of the Italian Grand Prix, belonged to Joshua Duerksen. The Invicta Racing driver won both races — a sweep, which is rare in a format designed to reshuffle the grid between them — and won the second of them by leading home a team-mate who happens to be Tsolov's closest rival for the title.

## The sprint: seventh to first

Duerksen started the sprint race seventh and finished it first. John Bennett took second and Oliver Goethe third. Tsolov brought his Campos Racing car home fifth, which is a points finish and, on a weekend where the outright pace was not there, a defensible return.

The win gave Duerksen the record for the most Formula 2 podium finishes at Monza. The circuit has been unusually kind to him, and the reason is the same one that makes Monza distinctive for every category that races there: with long straights and a heavy tow, track position on Saturday is worth less than it is almost anywhere else. A driver starting seventh at Monza is not in the position a driver starting seventh at Monaco is in.

## The feature race: an Invicta one-two

Sunday was the worse of the two days for the championship leader. Duerksen went back-to-back, and Rafael Câmara followed him across the line to complete an Invicta Racing one-two. It was Duerksen's fourth win of 2026 and his eighth in Formula 2, equalling the series record.

Tsolov finished sixth.

Two races, eleven points scored, and a direct rival who scored eighteen in the feature race alone. The arithmetic was not kind, and it did not need to be dramatic to hurt.

Eleven points. That is less than a single feature-race win, and considerably less than the margin Tsolov carried into the weekend.

Minì, thirty behind Câmara, is not yet out of it but needs help. Below him the gap widens sharply — Dunne is 59 points off the lead — which makes this a two-man championship with a third driver waiting for one of them to make a mistake.

## How Tsolov got here

The Bulgarian still holds the most wins of anyone in the field this season. The run that took him to the top of the table included a victory in the feature race at the Red Bull Ring and, a week later, a sprint win at Silverstone decided by a last-lap pass on Minì — Tsolov shadowed the Italian through the closing laps, attacked repeatedly, and waited for the mistake rather than forcing the issue into a place where neither car would finish.

That is the version of Tsolov that built the championship lead: quick when the car allowed it, patient when it did not. Monza was a weekend for the second half of that description, and the second half does not score enough points on its own.

## Why Monza was hard

Nothing about the weekend suggests a driver in trouble. Fifth and sixth on a circuit where the Campos car was not among the quickest is a competent weekend, and in most seasons it would be a perfectly acceptable one.

The problem is who was quick instead. When the two drivers ahead of you in the feature race are the runaway winner and the man second in the championship, a solid weekend costs more than an ordinary bad one. Câmara did not simply score; he scored where it mattered, and he did it from a car that finished first and second.

## What comes next

The season is heading into its closing rounds and the Bulgarian's task has changed shape. He arrived at Monza managing a championship lead. He leaves defending one, from a rival whose team currently has the fastest car in the field.

Eleven points means a single bad Sunday for Tsolov — a retirement, a penalty, a first-lap incident not of his making — would hand the lead over without Câmara needing to do anything special. It also means a single win would restore the cushion. Championships at this stage are decided less by pace than by which driver stops giving points away first.

Tsolov has led this championship for most of the season and has more wins than anyone chasing him. Neither of those facts is worth anything in November. What matters now is the next race, and the one after it, and whether the Campos car turns up quick at circuits that suit it better than Monza did.
